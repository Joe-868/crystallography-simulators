The 0D window is only about 0.5° wide in 2θ, so it looks like a thin line. Zoom in on the detector (mouse wheel or pinch) to see it clearly, and move the arm slowly: use the arrow keys on the slider for 0.1° steps.

The Soller plates pass only diffracted rays tilted less than α/2 out of the horizontal (equatorial) plane, so the steeply curved ends of a low-angle ring are blocked. Compare the 0D signal range near 39° with and without it. Here the sample is a point, so only the diffracted side can be shown; a real instrument can also use an incident-side Soller.

Crystal orientations are already random, so rotating a powder sample does not change the pattern. Swing the detector instead to catch higher-angle cones, as a diffractometer's 2θ arm does. 1D and 0D detectors only see part of each ring, so they are scanned in 2θ. The 1D strip is enlarged for visibility; the 0D window is about 0.5° wide in 2θ (real receiving slits are narrower, typically a few hundredths of a degree).
